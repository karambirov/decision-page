What the Team plan should cost per seat
How I counted

Seats and revenue: 14,900 seats on 2,140 Team accounts on October 31, 2026. Revenue is seats × price, as if every account paid the new price at once. In reality current customers and annual plans move later: see Who gets the new price.

Churn is applied every month to the seats that are left: after 12 months, 14,900 × (1 − churn)¹² seats remain. New customers are left out of every case, which is why revenue falls even at $12.

Churn at each price: in 2024 a 20% increase added 0.5 points for six months, so I added 0.25 points for every 10% of increase: 2.0% at $14, 2.2% at $15. I assumed the higher churn lasts the whole year, which is the cautious case. At $18 that rule gives 2.9%, but a 50% jump is far beyond what we have seen, so I took 4.0%.

New sign-ups at once, monthly plans after a notice, annual plans at renewal.

New customers have no old price to compare with, so they can start at the new one right away. Current customers need time to plan, and the first higher invoice is the moment most of them decide whether to stay. Annual customers have already paid for their year: changing it midway would cost more trust than it brings in.

The recommended plan: $15, 60 days' notice

  1. December 1. We tell current customers about the new price (how: the next question) and update the pricing page.
  2. December 1. New sign-ups see $15 and pay it from their first invoice.
  3. December and January. Current customers keep paying $12 during the 60-day notice. Support answers questions about the new totals.
  4. February 1. The first $15 invoice reaches 1,500 monthly accounts. This is when most cancellations happen.
  5. From February. Annual plans renew at $15, each after a reminder 30 days before the renewal.
  6. November 2027. The last annual plans renew, and every Team account pays $15.

What happens to annual plans

With a separate email to every admin, 60 days ahead, that shows their new bill.

In 2024 people complained more about the short notice than about the price itself. A notice in the app misses admins who rarely log in, and the product newsletter is opened too seldom to carry news like this. A plain email with the new price, the date, the reason and the customer's own new total answers most questions before they reach support.

A first draft of the email

Subject: The Team plan will cost $15 per seat from February 1

Hi {first name}, from February 1, 2027, the Team plan will cost $15 per seat a month instead of $12. For your {7} seats, that's ${105} a month instead of ${84}.

Why: running the service costs us more than it did at our last change in 2024. Since then we've also added {recurring invoices, card payments and payment reminders}.

If you pay yearly, your price stays the same until your next renewal. Questions? Reply to this email, and a person will answer.

    • New sign-ups: a higher price may also turn fewer trials into paying customers. I didn't look at that.
